## 5. Linear model against the nonlinear aircraft

| Airspeed | Settling time, linear / nonlinear (s) | Peak elevator, linear / nonlinear (deg) | Lowest airspeed in the step (m/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 m/s | 5.084 / 5.595 | 20.83 / 21.65 | 17.67 |
| 25 m/s | 4.137 / 4.369 | 11.18 / 11.18 | 22.57 |
| 32 m/s | 2.190 / 2.197 | 4.96 / 4.96 | 29.82 |

At low speed the airspeed decays during the step, so the elevator loses effectiveness and the nonlinear response is slower than the linear model predicts. A design verified only on the linear models misjudges its own settling time.

## 6. Why the range starts at 20 m/s

The steady elevator needed to hold +5 deg at fixed throttle, from each linear model's DC gain:

| Airspeed | Trim elevator (deg) | Change for +5 deg (deg) | Absolute (deg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 m/s | -20.81 | -7.00 | -27.81 |
| 20 m/s | -15.41 | -5.43 | -20.84 |
| 25 m/s | -7.10 | -2.82 | -9.92 |
| 32 m/s | -1.33 | -1.20 | -2.53 |

At 18 m/s the hold would need more than the 25 deg stop, whatever the gains: the step is physically out of reach there. At 20 m/s it needs about 21 deg, which leaves a few degrees for the transient.
